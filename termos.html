Terms of use

Before opening the chest, it is worth reading calmly what we agree on here.

Last updated on 26 October 2026 · Draft under legal review

Hello. This is Iara, the keeper of your family’s memories.

An agreement between Iara and your family

Iara is a digital chest for keeping a family’s history: photos, recordings, documents and the tree of those who came before. It is offered by Yasmim Seadi, registered under CNPJ no. 29.863.546/0001-58, based in Novo Hamburgo/RS, Brasil, which in this text we call “Iara”.

By creating an account or joining a family, you accept these terms. They explain what you can expect from Iara and what Iara expects from each person in the family. If anything is unclear, write to contact@iara.family before accepting.

How your data is handled is set out in the Privacy notice, which is part of this agreement.

Who can have an account

An account on Iara is for people aged 18 or over. When signing up, each person confirms their own age.

Children and teenagers are part of the family, but they do not have an account. They appear as a profile in the tree and in the photos, and the adults of the family look after those profiles. If Iara learns that an account belongs to someone under 18, that account is closed, and the memories that person kept remain in the family’s chest.

Each account is personal. To sign in, Iara sends a code to your email or uses Face ID, your fingerprint or your device’s unlock. Look after access to your email and your phone as you would look after the key to your home.

The family and the roles

Whoever creates the chest becomes the family’s Guardian. Other people join by invitation, and each invitation comes with a role. The role says what each person can do in the chest.

RoleWhat it can do
GuardianEverything the Admin does, and also export a copy of the chest
AdminInvite people, change roles, keep and edit memories and profiles
EditorKeep memories, create and edit profiles in the tree, comment and react
CommenterSee the chest, comment and react
ViewerSee the chest

The Guardian answers for the family before Iara: they choose who joins and with which role. Nobody can remove the Guardian from the chest. One family sees nothing of another family.

The memories belong to the family

Everything you keep in Iara remains yours and your family’s. Iara does not become the owner of any photo, recording, document, comment or information in the tree.

For the app to work, Iara needs a limited permission: to store the files, make backup copies, create thumbnails and show each memory only to the people in your family. This permission ends when the memory is deleted.

Iara never uses the memories for advertising, never sells them and never shows them to anyone outside the family. It also does not use the families’ content to train other companies’ computer programs.

What belongs in the chest

The chest is for the family’s history. When you keep a memory, you confirm that you have the right to share it with your family.

It is not allowed to keep or write:

  • content that exposes children or teenagers in an intimate or sexual way;
  • threats, harassment or insults against anyone;
  • other people’s content that you have no permission to use, such as whole books or films;
  • files made to damage devices or steal data.

The chest accepts photos, recordings, PDFs and images of documents, with size limits per file. If a rule is broken, Iara may remove the content and, in serious cases, close the account. When the law requires it, Iara informs the authorities.

The platform belongs to Iara

The memories belong to the family. The app, the name Iara, the symbol, the illustrations, the texts and the code belong to Yasmim Seadi and are protected by intellectual property law (Brasilian Laws no. 9.279/1996, 9.609/1998 and 9.610/1998).

Each person receives a personal, free and non-transferable licence to use Iara with their own family. This licence does not allow copying, reselling or taking the app apart, nor using the Iara brand.

When a rule is broken

It is also not allowed to try to enter another family’s chest, lend your own account, use robots to access the app or test its security without authorisation.

Depending on how serious it is, Iara may:

  • warn the person and ask them to correct it;
  • remove the content or suspend the account for a time;
  • close the account and, when the law requires it, inform the authorities.

Before closing an account, Iara tells the family’s Guardian, so that the memories kept by that person are not lost.

Who appears in the memories

A family’s history speaks of many people, including those without an account. So some care applies to everyone:

  • People who have passed away. Profiles of those who are gone can only be created and edited by the Guardian, Admins and Editors. Write about them with the respect the family expects.
  • Children and teenagers. The adults of the family answer for the profiles and photos of anyone under 18. Keep only what those responsible for them would agree to share with the family.
  • Living people without an account. Before tagging someone in a photo or creating a profile in the tree, think about whether that person would be comfortable. If they ask to be removed, the family should do so.

Anyone, with or without an account, can ask for a memory or a profile about them to be reviewed by writing to contact@iara.family. Those with an account can also use the Support screen, in Settings.

Security and where the memories are kept

The memories are kept in the European Union, on Scaleway servers in Paris, France. Iara’s emails are sent through Brevo, also French. We chose Europe because its data protection laws are among the strictest in the world, and no American company keeps the families’ data.

The files are kept in a private space and only open through temporary links, valid for 15 minutes. With every request, the database checks whether the person belongs to that family and what their role allows. Every sign-in, invitation and export is recorded.

As required by Brasil’s Internet Civil Framework (Marco Civil da Internet, Law no. 12.965/2014), Iara keeps access records for 6 months: the IP address, date and time of each sign-in. These records are only handed to an authority with a court order.

No system is perfect. If Iara learns of improper access affecting your family, it tells the Guardian and Brasil’s National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) within the legal deadlines.

Taking a copy, leaving and deleting

The Guardian can export the chest at any time, in Settings. The copy comes as a .zip file, with the photos, recordings and documents by year, a list of who appears in each memory, the comments and the tree in a format that other genealogy sites can open. Only the Guardian exports, because the copy leaves with everything inside.

You can leave a family or ask for your account to be deleted through the Support screen. When an account is deleted:

  • the access data (email, sessions, Face ID or fingerprint) is deleted;
  • the memories the person kept remain in the chest, because they are part of the family’s history, unless the person asks for them to be removed;
  • the comments remain without the name of whoever wrote them.

The Guardian can ask for the whole chest to be deleted. Before that, Iara offers the export. Deleted files also leave the backup copies within 30 days.

The rest of the agreement

Price. At launch, Iara is free. If there is ever a charge, Iara gives at least 30 days’ notice, and no family loses access to its own memories without first being able to export them.

Changes to these terms. When the terms change, Iara gives notice by email and in the app, at least 15 days before. If you do not agree, you can export the memories and close the account.

Limits. Iara works so that the chest is always available and the memories are safe, but there may be pauses for maintenance or failures beyond its control. That is why we recommend that the Guardian export a copy from time to time. Iara is not responsible for what the people of the family write or keep.

Law and jurisdiction. This agreement follows the laws of Brasil, including the General Data Protection Law (LGPD) and the Consumer Protection Code. The courts of Novo Hamburgo/RS are chosen, without prejudice to the consumer’s right to use the courts of their own place of residence.

Contact. Questions, requests and reports: the Support screen, in Settings, or contact@iara.family. The answer arrives in your email.
