Security

Security comes before everything

What belongs to the family stays with the family. And what does not exist yet, we say plainly.

  • No documents requested

    Iara never asks for identity papers, a phone number or an address. To take part you give a name and an email, and confirm you are 18 or over.

  • Only your family sees it

    Each family is closed. The rules live inside the database itself, which refuses to show a memory to anyone outside the family.

  • A private vault for files

    Photos, recordings and PDFs stay in a private vault. Each one opens only through a link that expires after 15 minutes.

  • Kept in Europe

    The servers are in Paris, under European data protection law. No big tech company holds the memories.

  • No ads

    Your family’s data is never sold or used for advertising.

  • No password to forget

    You sign in with a code sent to your email. It lasts 10 minutes, and you can sign out of every device at once.

About Iara

Iara began with my great-grandfather Milad, who left Lebanon for Brasil. On the way, his papers and memories were scattered. Gathering what was left showed me how much a family loses without noticing.

Studying International Relations, I saw that whoever controls digital infrastructure controls which stories survive. Iara is my answer: a sanctuary where each family keeps its own memory, under its own rules.

That is why I turned the letters AI around. From Artificial Intelligence to Ancestral Intelligence, the kind that passes from one generation to the next.

The name is my mother’s, Iara, who drew the shell, the key and the chest on this page.

Questions

What families ask before they begin

Does Iara ask for my documents?

No. To create an account you give your name, your email and how you would like to be addressed, and you confirm you are 18 or over. Nothing else is asked. Certificates, letters or old papers only enter the chest if your family decides to keep them.

Who can see what we keep?

Only the people your family invites, each according to their role. There are no public profiles, no feed and nothing that search engines can find.

Can the people who run Iara see our memories?

Files stay in a private vault and open only through temporary links, and the database refuses to show one family’s memories to anyone outside it. End-to-end encryption, in which not even Iara could open them, is planned for a later version. Until it exists, we do not claim it.

What happens to our memories if Iara closes?

They never depend on Iara alone. Whoever looks after the chest can download everything at any time, in common formats: photos, recordings and PDFs in folders by year, and the tree in GEDCOM, which FamilySearch, MyHeritage and Ancestry open.

Do I need to download an app?

No. Iara opens in the phone’s browser. You can add it to the home screen, and it opens like any other app.

Can relatives who have passed away be in the tree?

Yes. They join the tree without an account, with dates, places, nationalities and memories. Only the Guardian, Admins and Editors create and change these profiles.

Can children use Iara?

Accounts are for people aged 18 and over. Children appear in the tree and in photos, looked after by the adults of the family.

Which languages does Iara speak?

The app is in Portuguese for now, and this site is in English and Portuguese. More languages are on the way.

Can I leave a family or delete my account?

Yes, at any time. Leaving a family keeps your account. Deleting the account removes your access, and your comments stay in the chest without your name. A Guardian first passes the guardianship to someone else.
