Data governance

How Iara looks after the families’ data on the inside, every day, and what it does when something goes wrong.

Last updated on 26 October 2026 · Draft under legal review

Hello. This policy is Iara’s promise about how it looks after the families’ data on the inside. The Privacy notice says what is kept. This page says how that care happens every day, and what to do when something goes wrong.

It applies to Yasmim Seadi (CNPJ 29.863.546/0001-58), to whoever comes to work on Iara and to the companies that provide services to it.

1. What Iara does and what it never does

Iara doesIara never does
Keeps the memories for the family and only for itSell, rent or lend data
Asks only for what the app needs to workShow advertising or build profiles for advertising
Lets the family take a complete copy whenever it wantsUse the memories to train computer programs
Explains each use of data in plain wordsOpen a family’s memories without its request
Deletes what is no longer needed, within the agreed deadlinesShow one family’s chest to another

When support needs to look at something inside a chest to solve a problem, Iara first asks the person who wrote for permission, and looks only at what is needed.

2. Who looks after what

WhoResponsibility

A new supplier only comes in after Iara checks where it keeps the data and how it protects it. The current list is always in the Privacy notice.

3. How the data is protected

  • Separation between families. The database itself checks, on every read, whether the person belongs to that family. A mistake in the app is not enough to open another family’s chest.
  • Sign-in without a password. A one-time code by email, which expires in minutes, or Face ID and fingerprint through the WebAuthn standard. Iara keeps only the device’s public key.
  • Encrypted connection. All traffic between the phone and Iara goes through HTTPS.
  • Least possible access. The app runs with limited permissions in the database. Notices, for example, are only created inside the database, and nobody can create a false notice for someone else.
  • Access records. IP, date and time are kept for 6 months, in a place separate from the memories, and then deleted.
  • Backup copies. Made every day and kept in the European Union, deleted within 30 days.
  • Security review. Before launch and then after every major change to the app.

4. When something goes wrong

An incident is any access, loss or leak of data that should not have happened. Iara follows these steps:

  • Contain, at once: close the flaw, end suspicious sessions and remove access.
  • Understand, within 24 hours: what happened, which families and which data were affected.
  • Inform, within 3 working days: the people affected, by email and in the app, and the ANPD when there is a relevant risk (ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD no. 15/2024). The notice says what happened, what was affected and what the person can do.
  • Recover: restore what was lost from the backup copies.
  • Learn: record the incident, what was done and what changes so that it does not happen again. The record is kept for 5 years.

Anyone who notices something strange can report it through support or contact@iara.family, at any time.
