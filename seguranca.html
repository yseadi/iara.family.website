Security

Your family’s memories are not a product.

How Iara protects what your family keeps, and what it does not do yet.

Iara never asks for identity documents. Not a passport, not an ID card, not a phone number, not an address.

What we ask to create an account

  • Your name
  • Your email, to receive the sign-in code
  • How you would like to be addressed
  • Confirmation that you are 18 or over

What we never ask

  • Identity documents or passports
  • Phone number or address
  • Passwords
  • Access to your contacts or your phone’s photos

Certificates, letters and old papers only enter the chest if your family decides to keep them, one by one, and they are seen only by that family. Nothing in Iara depends on them.

01

Who sees what

Each family is closed

There are no public profiles, no feed and no page that a search engine can find. A family’s memories are seen only by the people that family invites, and each one sees and does what their role allows.

The rules also live inside the database itself, which refuses to show a family’s memories to anyone outside it, even if the app makes a mistake. The app connects to the database with a restricted user, who does not own the tables and cannot bypass those rules.

02

How it is protected

Layer by layer

These are the layers that exist today, in the order a memory passes through them.

  • An encrypted connectionEvery page, every photo and every recording travels between your phone and Iara over HTTPS. The connection between the app and the database is encrypted too, and checks the database’s certificate.
  • A private vault for filesPhotos, recordings and PDFs are kept in a private storage space in Paris. Nothing there is public. Each file opens only through a signed link that expires after 15 minutes.
  • Only what a memory needsThe chest accepts photos, audio and PDFs. Other kinds of files are refused, so nothing that could run on someone’s phone enters the family’s space.
  • Rules inside the databaseEach person reads only the families they belong to and changes only what their role allows. These rules are checked by automated tests against a real database.
  • Sign-in without a passwordYou sign in with a 6-digit code sent to your email. It lasts 10 minutes and allows 5 attempts, and Iara stores only a fingerprint of it, never the code itself. A session lasts 30 days, and you can sign out of every device at once.
  • Invitations that expireEach invitation link works for one person and lasts 7 days. Whoever sent it can cancel it before it is used.
03

Where it lives

In Paris, with European companies

The app, the database and the files are hosted by Scaleway, a French company, in its data centres in Paris. Sign-in codes are sent by Brevo, also French. No American company stores your family’s data.

The data is protected by the European General Data Protection Regulation and by Brasil’s LGPD. The source code is kept on GitHub, an American service, but no family data ever goes there.

Why we chose Europe

04

What we keep about you

Only what the law requires, for as long as it requires

Brasilian law, the Marco Civil da Internet, requires apps like Iara to keep the IP address, date and time of each sign-in for six months. Iara keeps exactly that. The app itself cannot read these records; they come out only by court order, and are erased automatically after six months, even if the account no longer exists.

There are no trackers, no advertising cookies and no visit counters, in the app or on this site. The only cookies are the ones the app needs to work, such as the one that keeps you signed in.

05

What never happens

Memories are not merchandise

  • Never soldYour family’s data is not sold, rented or shared with anyone.
  • Never used for advertisingThere are no ads in Iara, and no profile of you is built to sell anything.
  • Never used to train artificial intelligencePhotos, voices and documents are not used to train any artificial intelligence model.
06

You stay in control

Leave whenever you want, with everything

  • Take the whole chestWhoever looks after the family downloads everything in one file: photos, recordings and PDFs in folders by year, and the tree in GEDCOM, the format that FamilySearch, MyHeritage and Ancestry open.
  • Leave a familyYou can leave a family at any time. Your account stays, and so do the memories you shared.
  • Delete your accountDeleting the account removes your access and your personal data. Your comments stay in the chest, without your name, so the family’s conversation is not broken. A chest in which you were the only person is deleted together with its files.
07

What Iara does not do yet

Said plainly

These are the limits of Iara today, and what is planned for each one.

  • End-to-end encryptionToday the files are protected by the layers above, but they are not yet sealed in a way that not even the people who run Iara could open. End-to-end encryption, with keys that belong to each family, is planned for a later version.
  • The place where a photo was takenPhotos keep the information the phone saved with them, which can include the place. If you prefer, remove the location before sending. Removing it automatically is planned.
  • Recovery without emailFor now, access to the account depends on your email. Recovery confirmed by two relatives and a key written on paper is planned.
  • An independent reviewBefore the first families arrive, an independent developer reviews the access rules, the sign-in and the invitations. Daily backups of the database, and a copy of the files in another European region, are switched on at the same time.

Reporting a vulnerability

If you find a flaw, we want to hear it first

If you are a researcher, a developer or simply someone who noticed something strange, this is how to tell us, and what you can expect in return.

I

How to report

Write to contact@iara.family with the word Security in the subject. Describe what you found, the steps to see it again and, if you can, what it could expose.

II

What we ask

Do not open, copy or change memories that belong to another family. Use your own test account, and give us time to fix the problem before telling anyone else.

III

What we commit to

A person reads every report, answers you and keeps you informed until the problem is fixed. If a family was affected, we tell that family plainly. If you want, your name goes on our thank-you list.
