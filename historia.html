About Iara

Memory is not disposable data, but a sovereign right and the deepest form of resistance.

Who writes

Yasmim Seadi

I am Brasilian, with Lebanese roots. I studied International Relations and researched the geopolitical impact of technology, and one thing became clear to me: whoever controls digital infrastructure controls which stories survive.

I watched whole villages disappear from digital maps, and algorithms that quietly push some voices out of sight. Families facing war, forced displacement and climate disasters lose, along with their homes, the photographs, the papers and the voices that told them who they were.

In Southern Lebanon and in Gaza, I watched towns wiped from digital maps. Against that, communities have an old practice called counter-mapping: drawing their own borders, naming their own villages and claiming their existence on their own terms. Iara is a counter-map for families.

I decided to build an alternative: a place where a family keeps its own memory, under its own rules, far from the platforms that treat that memory as a product.

Where it began

Milad

In a world where wars, forced displacement, climate crises and digital monopolies erase whole villages from the map and silence whole stories, I refuse to forget. Inspired by recovering the path of my great-grandfather Milad, whose records and memory were scattered over time after he migrated from Lebanon to Brasil, I created this platform as a vault built to last.

Like so many who crossed the ocean, he brought almost nothing: a few photographs, an instrument, a clarinet, and the stories of his land, which were never written down.

The letters AI

Intelligence

Inteligência

learns from patternslearns from a bloodline

I deliberately turned the letters AI from Artificial Intelligence into Ancestral Intelligence. My purpose is to put technology, kept in Europe and away from the big tech companies, at the service of history and of the voices that came before us. So that no story is told by outside empires, and so that no lineage is erased from time.

Ancestral intelligence is what passes from one generation to the next: a recipe, a way of saying a name, the route a family took across the sea. Nobody trains it; it is learned at the table, in the kitchen and in the stories told before sleep.

Across the Global South, there have always been people who keep it: the griots of West Africa, the storytellers of the villages, the grandmothers. When books were burned and land was taken, history survived in their voices. Iara is built in their image, to listen, keep and pass on.

The name

Iara

The name holds three layers, one inside the other, like the rings on the water when a stone falls in.

  1. I

    My mother

    Iara is my mother’s name. Lending it to this platform is a tribute to her strength, to the protection that passes from mother to child and to the bond of our lineage.

  2. II

    The lady of the waters

    In Brasil, Iara is the figure of Indigenous legend who guards the rivers, a force of nature that protects her territory.

  3. III

    The Mitochondrial Eve

    The most recent common ancestor of every living person through the maternal line. An unbroken chain, from mother to child, since the beginning.

The symbols

Drawn by my mother

The shell, the key and the chest on these pages are drawn by hand by Iara, my mother. The trident joined them as the sign of protection.

  • The trident

    Protection. Instead of the cold padlock of technology, the weapon and the shield of the waters, guarding what flows through them.

  • The shell

    The voices. Just as one holds a shell to the ear to hear the sea, here the family listens to the voices of those who came before. A shell, and a shelter.

  • The key

    Hope. It is inspired by the keys Palestinian families still keep, as a sign of the right to return home.

  • The chest

    Preservation. The treasure at the bottom of the sea, where a family’s heritage stays untouched by war, by time and by distance.

With gratitude

Everything I keep, I learned from them.

To my parents,

for their unconditional love, for the safe ground beneath me and for teaching me that the greatest wealth a human being can carry is their integrity and their story. To my mother, Iara, lady of my waters and of my life, whose name I lend to this platform as a tribute to her strength, to matrilineal protection and to the sacred bond of our lineage.

A message in a bottle

Someone throws a story into the vast ocean, trusting that it will survive the tides and be found by whoever comes later. Every memory kept in Iara is a little like that: written today, for someone who has not been born yet.

To my ancestors,

to my great-grandfather Milad, to those who crossed oceans, to those who endured in distant lands and to those whose names time tried to erase. I honour every step, every pain, every crossing and every memory held in my genes. I am the dream and the living archive of you all.

To my future descendants,

I write this code and raise this digital sanctuary for you. So that you never have to search in the dark for who you are, where you came from or the weight of the land you carry on your feet. This vault is the promise that our roots will remain untouched, alive and safe from any storm.
